# Workshop content to abstract graphics

Record concrete source evidence before choosing shapes and colors: material/process, participant actions, learning output and mood. Keep source-specific palette reasons; example cyanotype blue is not a universal default.

Examples of inference directions:
- Cyanotype exposure/rinse: vivid blue exposure band and projected negative space, without a literal leaf.
- Clay pressing/kneading: saturated terracotta/red curves and compressed rounded surfaces.
- Weaving: smooth interlacing bands with source-related saturated thread colors.
- Sound sampling/layering: curved resonance, spacing and flowing overlaps, without a literal microphone icon.

Prefer one continuous large curve or surface with one optional soft auxiliary layer, middle/lower focus, asymmetry and rounded negative space. Target18–35% coverage as a starting point. Keep white canvas; choose vivid saturated hues that can support dark enough intersections for white glyphs. Do not copy brands, scatter generic icons or depict full literal objects.

No angular fragments, wedges, folded corners or sharp cutouts. Rounded SVG joins alone do not ensure flow. Make adjoining curve tangents and curvature continuous and inspect inner/outer contours; a turn radius below half the thick band's width can create a cusp. Use broad natural cubic/B-spline curves or verified Bézier controls. Allow cropping at canvas edges, not jagged internal breaks.

For controllable curves use SVG; for organic bitmap art use appropriate image-generation/editing tools. Ask for a text-free graphic based on the actual source, no QR/logos/lettering, no pale colors beneath text. Formal English comes from outlined typography. Check actual colors and opacity against white: at least4.5 contrast for white overlap text; preserve gray outside. Reposition or darken pale intersections within the theme rather than cutting white boxes out of the graphic.
