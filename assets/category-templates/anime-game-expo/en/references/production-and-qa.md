# Production, language adaptation and review

Generate a key visual without formal information, then add exact copy using controllable typesetting. Repair layout before regenerating an already accepted visual. Use real assets for logos, brand lettering and QR codes; do not ask an image model to invent them.

Establish priorities for title, event format, attendance decisions and CTA. Group artists, programmes or zones by their actual relationships instead of shrinking all text. Test fonts with actual titles, names, long English lines and numerals. Evaluate visible glyph edges, optical mass, title/subtitle relationship and image focal points. Mixed fonts are not inherently wrong: diagnose the relationship before forcing one family.

Expressive lettering may serve the title; functional details retain stable shapes and spacing. Decoration must not carry required facts. Keep mandatory copy away from noisy textures, faces, bright light and fine-line interference. Language editions share facts and priorities but may use different line groups, sizes and composition. Break English by semantic phrases; protect names and phrases in both languages. Do not call a machine-generated translation official.

Recompose each ratio instead of stretching or cutting required facts. Inspect actual reduced digital previews. Print settings come from the vendor; pixels do not establish bleed or CMYK requirements.

## Technical checks

Compare every required field, punctuation, date, time, tier, URL, QR target, name and quantity against the master. Check glyph coverage, overflow, actual contrast, safe areas and exported files. Reproducing a supplied fact does not independently verify it. The font selector verifies compatibility, not aesthetic quality.

## Visual checks

Review event/subtype clarity, visible attraction, hierarchy, title/subtitle harmony, font/subject fit, findable functional information and cultural/IP/media identity separately. A three-second check is a design prompt, not a measured audience result.

Critical errors include wrong facts, identity misrepresentation, missing essential glyphs, unreadable required copy, unresolved core rights or incorrect output specification; they block final delivery. Major problems weaken composition or understanding and require repair or explicit acceptance. Minor issues are optional improvements.

Store raw feedback separately from causal hypotheses. Do not convert “unnatural arrangement” into a prohibition of mixed fonts. Return to the affected stage; after two failed local repairs, revisit the upstream decision. Retain the previous version when the user prefers it.

Rule states: proposed / trial / adopted / rejected. Transfer evidence: not-tested / supported / contradicted. Case interpretation and technical checks do not establish visual approval. Deliver review candidates until the user confirms final direction and cultural suitability. Publish only when requested.
