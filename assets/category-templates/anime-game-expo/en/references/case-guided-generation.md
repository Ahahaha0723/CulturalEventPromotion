# Anime/game posters: transfer key visuals, style and typography from three cases

## Required production use
Read this file before choosing concepts, image prompts and typography for this category, and inspect the linked originals. Prioritize the three references selected by the user in this iteration; choose a suitable lead reference and supporting references from the actual event. New user references or a narrower requested scope take precedence.

For **key-visual effect, visual style and typography**, record observed mechanism → fit to this brief → original production action → output comparison. “Trendy”, a palette swap or a font swap alone is insufficient. Replace source characters, places and facts with this event's content. Connect subject, material and type through one proposition.

## Case A: Manga Barcelona (潮流-022)
Original: [P700.jpg](../assets/reference-posters/P700.jpg). The user's first attachment has the same SHA-256. The source library records high consensus, not demonstrated audience success for future outputs.
- **Key-visual effect:** an ensemble leans in from the sides and bottom like a group photograph. Enlarged foreground gestures, cropped figures and central cathedral towers establish depth and city identity; a black bat adds playfulness. Gaze and action invite participation. Relationships, not a pile of character cutouts, create the social effect.
- **Style:** lively manga contours with thin, watercolor-like color areas; bright sky blue and white, warm golden architecture, and red accents linking the cap and event mark. This describes appearance, not an established production technique.
- **Typography:** illustration occupies roughly the upper four fifths; a separate white band holds the red custom geometric MANGA wordmark, a stacked city name and anniversary mark. A heavy date line follows, with smaller centered venue and website. Reading: ensemble → identity → date → venue/site. The city-name split belongs to that specific logo lockup, not a universal line-break rule.
- **Adaptation:** suitable for broad anime conventions, city anime festivals and fan gatherings. Build an original/permitted ensemble and a relevant city/environment relationship. Use depth, crop and interaction; size the information band from actual copy and canvas. Borrow geometric construction for an original title, and retain supplied official marks as assets.
- **Review:** coherent gaze/action, relevant city/content, and a complete identity/date/venue sequence. Avoid unrelated characters, arbitrary landmarks and copy across faces.

## Case B: Arcane World (潮流-052)
Original: [P705.jpg](../assets/reference-posters/P705.jpg). High-consensus single-IP experience evidence; not direct validation of a broad game expo.
- **Key-visual effect:** a frontal blue-haired portrait fills the square, cropping hair, head and shoulders. Pink-purple eyes stand out against blue/black. The huge white title overlays shoulders and dark torso while sparing the face: character for emotion/IP recognition, title for event recognition.
- **Style:** painterly facial planes against blue/black/white torn-paper collage, grain and wear. Fine face versus coarse perimeter and a concentrated palette with a small magenta accent produce tension and rebellious street character. Blue, tears and neon are not game-category defaults.
- **Typography:** one massive white, tall condensed, heavy uppercase sans-serif title spans most of the width. Distress and a right-end drip make it material. Smaller centered venue/date follow; four white brand marks form a horizontal footer. Eye → title → venue/date → brands. Thai, Latin and logos have distinct roles; the source is not an English-language content master.
- **Adaptation:** appropriate to a confirmed single IP, character or work-led experience. Use permitted/original imagery, deliberate gaze/crop and a dark title surface. Select actual condensed fonts; keep textured lettering legible and functional copy clean. Recompose Chinese from its real glyphs and title length rather than squeezing it horizontally. A broad multi-IP expo needs its own representation and zone logic.
- **Review:** eyes and title remain visible together, texture has a purpose, and event format/date/venue are easy to find. Do not import source characters, logos or calendar facts.

## Case C: NYCC · Artist Alley Table F20 (潮流-053)
Original: [P96.jpg](../assets/reference-posters/P96.jpg). Selected by this user; the original library class remains a relatively high-rated reference with disagreement. This is a creator-table announcement, not the whole convention's official campaign.
- **Key-visual effect:** leaning figures and centrally joined hands establish a story; a large yellow disc stabilizes the page. A film-like ribbon, birds, miniature city and bursts cross scales, while a leg/shoe extends depth and motion. A compact personal world rewards closer viewing.
- **Style:** colorful narrative illustration with visible brush/grain character. Purple/yellow cool-warm contrast, blue clothing, orange-red skin, green foliage and cyan details support an individual identity. Sportswear, urban life and fantasy are source observations, not mandatory objects.
- **Typography:** small widely tracked convention name at top, then a larger widely tracked italic-serif creator name. Lower-left Artist Alley, heavy italic Table F20 and enormous heavy NYCC establish successive levels; small right-aligned date/address occupy the lower-right. Type overlays illustration with distinct weights, scales, angles and positions. Dense corner copy is a thumbnail limitation to improve, not a readability model.
- **Adaptation:** best for creator/fan-art zones and actual booth announcements. Lead with the creator's own/permitted work and its narrative identity. Make the real table code actionable; keep host-event name and creator separate. Compare a real italic serif for the English signature and a real bold italic sans for table information; Chinese can recompose the corresponding reading structure.
- **Review:** identifiable creator, host event and table; natural type/image junction; readable small date/address. Do not invent a booth code or replace a whole expo's full name with an acronym merely to resemble this case.

## Choose and combine by purpose
1. Broad anime convention/city gathering: A; existing014 can support dense functional modules.
2. Single-IP experience: B, with both character identity and event format established.
3. Creator/artist-table announcement: C, prioritizing work and accurate location.
4. Broad game expo: derive choices from the games, play/testing and zones. A's participation, B's focused anchor or C's original storytelling may transfer locally. These are not a fully validated broad-game-expo training set; missing044 remains pending-case.

Different references may serve image, material and typography roles if one proposition connects them. For example, an original illustration exhibition may combine C's storytelling with A's separate information band. Do not pile the ensemble, portrait and booth typography together. Color, count, ratio, direction and fonts depend on the brief.

## Carry into production and review
- Supply selected originals to an image tool when reference input is supported, specifying composition/material roles. Otherwise record that images were not passed and use detailed observations. Naming a reference alone is insufficient. Formal small copy remains controlled typography.
- Prompts specify this event's content anchor, subject relationships/crop, focus/depth, materials/palette, title junction and functional information area. Source cities, IP, dates and table codes never fill missing brief facts.
- Use [font roles](font-routing.md) and actual bilingual text. Compare optical scale, visible edges, spacing and image mass; no fixed title/subtitle ratio. Mixed families can work. Tracking does not excuse broken words, and Chinese does not inherit Latin-letter splits.
- Compare outputs with the lead original across all three dimensions at full and actual thumbnail sizes. Record implemented mechanisms, gaps, accepted layers and repair scope. Glyph/export success is not aesthetic approval.
- Keep accepted layers. Text-only feedback returns to typography; visual mismatch returns to subject/composition/material rather than random font/color changes. Existing pre-generation decisions and final human review still apply; inherit explicit decisions.
