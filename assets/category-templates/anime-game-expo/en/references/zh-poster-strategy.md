# Chinese-language poster strategy

Decide whether the actual Chinese title is one unit or can justifiably separate into a title and explanatory line. Short titles can act as imagery; long names need more than large size. Protect names, works, IP and venues. Punctuation comes from the master or an approved display override; do not automatically add colons, quotes or book-title brackets.

Two families or sizes in a subtitle may be valid. Examine visible edges, total width, optical mass and vertical space before selecting alignment, centering, offset or correspondence. Do not force a two-group mapping without a natural composition. Chinese serif and sans have different apparent em boxes; equal baseline coordinates do not ensure optical alignment.

Expressive title candidates coexist with distinct roles for format, lineup/performers/zones, dates/venue and CTA. Avoid decorative essentials or faint/tiny facts. Group a genuine long list or create a detail edition instead of deleting names.

Test numerals separately: dates, times, prices, tiers and table/work numbers must retain year, unit and session meaning. Distinguish doors/show/closing. Do not infer the new year from a reference format. Size and spacing depend on the actual viewing scale.

Follow font-routing.md. Compare real Chinese titles and number specimens, then check every glyph. Preserve approved simplified/traditional forms and names; missing glyphs do not justify renaming people. Retain official Latin artist/work/character/brand spelling in mixed text.

Chinese can use a more compact composition than English while keeping the same facts and priorities. Inspect actual exports and reduced previews; copy checks do not approve typography or harmony.

## Strict font/layout matching

Before generation or text repair, read [best-fitting reference typography](strict-reference-typography.md), inspect images, compare real samples and lock roles/layout. Candidate order is not aesthetic fit; added italics/families or layout changes need source/brief evidence.
