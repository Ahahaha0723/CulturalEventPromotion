# Turn excellent references into this project's design

Read before selecting concepts, fonts and generation prompts when the user supplies favorite posters or asks for a result closer to an exemplar. Start with the images named in the current request; supplement with relevant bundled cases. Read the actual images, not just ratings, filenames or candidate-font tables. Without new references, select suitable subtype cases rather than defaulting to the same image.

## Observe and select

Inspect originals and label each role: composition, imagery, material, title construction or typography. Separate visible evidence, inference and unknowns. Missing images cannot support a claimed visual or font analysis. Preserve source rating tiers, disagreements and adjacent-category status. Usually choose one primary reference and supporting references only where useful; do not combine incompatible visual languages.

Observe decisions that affect production: first focal point, subject scale, contrast blocks, negative space and reading path; the image's relation to performers, place, sound, program, gameplay or cultural content; title strokes, width, optical weight, tracking, texture and custom alteration; title/subtitle/image/date relationships; and the role of grain, print, lines, photography or illustration without losing small-copy legibility.

Record a concise chain: reference → observed mechanism → why it fits this brief → original adaptation → prompt/layout action → review criterion. Use ../assets/reference-learning-template.json if useful, or merge it into the prompt plan. Avoid paperwork for its own sake. Words such as premium, trendy and reference style are not executable design decisions. State the limited borrowing scope when a favorite case does not otherwise fit.

## Carry the analysis into production

If the image tool supports reference inputs, pass the selected images and label each purpose. Merely naming an image in a prompt does not establish that it was supplied. Otherwise describe concrete observations, record that the image was not passed, and do not claim strict style matching. Specify focal subject, regions, materials, contrast and text space, replacing source motifs with this brief's own content anchors.

Typography also borrows relationships: whether the title acts as a graphic, how text meets the subject, and where decision information belongs. Do not mechanically place a generic bold heading over carefully designed imagery. Chinese and English share the proposition and material language, but recompose around actual text length and semantic groups. Do not use horizontal squeezing, arbitrary word fragments or tiny text as a substitute for style.

Do not carry over source event facts, artist billing, official logos, IP characters, identity or unlicensed artwork. Even same-category mechanisms require a content-based reason. Monochrome, metal lettering, neon, serif and close-up portraits are not defaults for every event.

## Title construction and real fonts

Choose real-font typesetting, editable original letterform construction, or an original generated title graphic according to the brief. Functional text remains controlled typesetting with bundled binaries, glyph coverage and recorded fallback. Unknown source families stay unknown; observed appearance and an open candidate are not a confirmed identity or a reproduction of a custom wordmark.

A generated title is a bounded exception for expressive ORIGINAL review artwork, never a replacement for an existing official logo or brand wordmark. Specify exact title, language and permitted alteration; leave all other formal copy for typesetting. Inspect every character and thumbnail readability. Repair the title layer for omissions, malformed or ambiguous letters; use a real font or editable letterforms where necessary. Do not present it as an installable font or official identity. Final inclusion requires character review, aesthetic acceptance and the existing release conditions. The default remains a key visual without formal lettering; this exception never covers dates, venues, ticketing, organizers, logos or QR codes.

## Compare and iterate

Compare the composed result with selected references at full and target thumbnail sizes. Are the borrowed composition/material/letterform mechanisms actually visible? Do title, subtitle and image connect? Is the subject specific to this brief? Is decision information legible? Copy, glyph and export checks do not establish aesthetic success. Never invent similarity scores, audience approval or effectiveness.

When the user reports a large gap from the exemplars, diagnose concept, subject, composition, title or material and return to the affected layer. Changing only font, palette or resolution cannot repair a weak proposition or image/type relationship. Retain accepted parts and record raw feedback, scope and outcome. A positively received example supports a conditional rule, not validation across all categories.
