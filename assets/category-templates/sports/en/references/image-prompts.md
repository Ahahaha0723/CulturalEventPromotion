# Landmark deconstruction and gradient-paper image prompts

Generate hero and background separately. Add actual English glyphs and real logos later. Fill every placeholder with the current sport, cues, colors, region and ratio before calling an image tool. Save final hero_prompt/background_prompt plus reference and asset paths.

## 1 Sporting landmark extraction and hero prompt

Select one sporting landmark from upstream sport/theme and record two visible structural/action clues. Basketball seams plus a shot path; tennis frame/mesh plus strike direction; running tread/lane rhythm plus stride; swimming lanes/ripple plus propulsion. Do not use a trophy, mascot or brand mark without a source basis.

**Template**

> Create a text-free sporting hero for [sport and theme], based on [specific equipment/field/action landmark], supported by [two source cues]. Retain [partial recognizable structural cues] so the sport is identifiable. Use flat experimental print collage:4–6 clearly separated major irregular paper pieces, local tears, displaced overlaps and large transparent negative spaces. Break the intact closed photographic silhouette; recognition comes from partial seams/mesh/structural pieces, not an unbroken realistic object. A few small fragments move along [direction] to express [speed/strength/team tension]. Avoid a generic intact equipment icon or unrecognizable debris.
>
> Use only the background gradient's shared hue family [principal/secondary colors and their light/mid/dark variants]. Do not retain natural equipment colors that conflict with that palette. Separate pieces through flat luminance/saturation differences, with small subordinate neutral paper edges and structural lines. Each piece uses a single flat color or limited two-color black-and-white halftone, screen-print grain and light paper wear. These are print textures, not3D lighting. Prohibit realistic sphere volume, photographic highlights, smooth shading gradients, specular reflection, thick cast shadows and plastic/metal rendering.
>
> Slightly tilt the hero in [actual upper-middle region]. Clearly enlarge the visible hero and associated motion:75–85% of canvas width for central or80–90% for bottom-band, after trimming useless transparent padding. Keep at least60px from title/date. Preserve proportions; do not squeeze the landmark or leave a tiny object on a large transparent canvas. Output a separate transparent-background hero, including transparent negative cutouts. No letters, numbers, logos, watermark, QR codes, unrelated equipment or dense tiny fragments.

Use the available image tool's transparency setting for a cutout. Inspect local references before supplying them. Preserve material/deconstruction relationships while replacing unrelated sports, reference words and brands; never transplant Nike or another logo into a new event.

**Basketball example**

> Generate a large text-free transparent hero for a3-on-3 basketball event. Use partial basketball seams, surface arcs and a few court-line fragments as sporting cues. Rearrange them into4–6 clearly separated flat paper planes with irregular cuts, tears, offset overlaps and transparent negative spaces, breaking the complete ball silhouette. A few small pieces follow an upward-right shot trajectory. Hero colors share the background:forest/emerald/mint for a green background, deep blue/purple-blue/pale purple for blue-purple; no default orange ball. Neutral seams and paper edges are minor. Use flat halftone and screen-print grain, not realistic lighting or spherical volume. Keep visible width near80% with trimmed padding, normal proportions and no formal text, logo, watermark or3D material.

**Tennis example**

> Generate a large text-free transparent tennis hero, slightly tilted. Partial oval frame, crossing strings and grip fragments establish recognition. Split them into unequal flat paper pieces with irregular tears, displaced overlaps and transparent openings; small fragments share strike direction. Do not retain an intact photographic racket silhouette. Use the same principal hue family as the background, with flat light/dark variation and minor neutral paper/string accents. Black-and-white halftone, photocopy grain and light print wear create a flat deconstructed collage. No text, logo, watermark, QR code, specular material or smooth3D shading; do not scatter the structure beyond recognition.

Colors must follow the new background. Blue-purple/green examples are not fixed defaults. The enlarged actual landmark occupies the main upper-middle space, not a small sticker.

## 2 Halftone and paper-textured gradient prompt

**Template**

> Make a text-free sports-poster background for [theme], [actual pixel size and ratio]. Fill the whole canvas with [2–3 vivid saturated principal hues and their thematic reason] in a smooth [vertical/diagonal/radial] gradient, expressing [speed/strength/energy]. Add one soft glow near [hero region]. The later title/subtitle/slogan will be white#FFFFFF and other text#F1FFF7:keep their zones stable and dark enough, without overexposed patches. Do not generate those words.
>
> Blend subtle black-and-white halftone dots, fine photocopy grain, light paper fibers and natural print wear over the color. Dot density varies with value while retaining saturated principal hues, not converting the image to grayscale. Hero colors will use the same light/mid/dark hue family, so add no unrelated new palette. Texture is natural, even and subordinate; reduce contrast near title/date/small-text zones without an obvious blank rectangle. Fill the entire canvas; no hard bands, dirty stains, cracks, large white patches, moiré or many bright flares. No text, numbers, logo, watermark, QR code, person, equipment or extra central subject.

**Blue-purple example**

> Create a4:5 vertical sports background,2400×3000px. Deep blue, electric blue and vivid purple form a lively top-to-bottom gradient, saturated and smooth, with a small soft glow near the hero. Keep lower title/date areas dark enough for white and pale-mint lettering. Mix subtle black-and-white halftone, photocopy grain and fine paper fibers across the color, with light screen-print texture; variable dot density does not obscure blue-purple or create large gray-white blocks. Text zones have gentler texture while the full background stays continuous. No words, logos, watermark, equipment, people, additional central graphics, coarse noise, moiré or hard color bands.

Generate background as nontransparent. Follow supplied brand/theme instead of blindly choosing blue-purple. Later overlay true glyphs/logos above texture. Inspect full size and thumbnails for contrast and moiré.

## Strong flat-deconstruction constraint

> Use2D printed collage, enlarged. Clearly separate4–6 flat paper planes with offset assembly and large negative spaces. Retain only seam/string/partial-structure sporting cues; break the realistic closed shape. Use single or limited two-color halftone per plane. No realistic volume, smooth lighting gradients, highlights, reflection, heavy shadow or photographic texture. Do not generate an intact3D basketball and merely add torn seams. Share the background's hue family, keep proportions, avoid many tiny fragments.

## Output checks

- Landmark choice and two cues belong to the sport; this is not equipment photography or a generic glow ring.
- Scale reaches the visible target and shares background hues with luminance separation; no large conflicting natural equipment color.
- Cuts/offsets/negative space have hierarchy and a common motion; cutouts are transparent, with no reference words/brands.
- Background remains saturated color; halftone/paper are texture, with low interference near type.
- Canvas/region match, real English type stays sharp, and generated words are not used as formal typography.
- Revise hero structure or background texture separately rather than repeatedly replacing the whole poster and losing locked typography.
