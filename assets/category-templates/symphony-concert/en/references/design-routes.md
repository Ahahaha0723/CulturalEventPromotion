Colour and materials follow the programme. Contemporary photography and sans type can serve Chinese opera; light natural colour and abstraction can serve symphony. Calligraphy, serif, red and gold do not establish cultural accuracy. Use sourced and reviewed masks, costumes, patterns, props and people.

Compare same-family and contrasting title/subtitle compositions. Evaluate brush flow, optical edges, gesture direction and information balance together. Group long programmes by composer/work/performer role; main poster and programme detail share one facts version.

Test at least two candidate typefaces using the actual title at full, target and thumbnail sizes. Vertical Chinese is conditional for short titles; dates, tickets, programmes and English body copy stay conventionally readable. Protect names, catalogue numbers, keys and ensemble names as semantic units. Material texture must not damage reading.

Prompt plan: performance form → work/person identity → verifiable cultural anchor → visual rhythm → copy space → cultural decisions. Opera and Xiangsheng strategies remain trial/pending-case until matching examples are supplied; dance/fusion examples are not substitutes for direct evidence.
