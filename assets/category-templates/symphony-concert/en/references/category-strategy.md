# Traditional and classical performance: category strategy

This is a practical design group, not one cultural identity. Use separate modules for Chinese opera, Xiangsheng and symphony. Identify Chinese music, dance drama and fusion accurately before borrowing mechanisms. Traditional does not mean costume/red/gold; symphony does not require a golden instrument on black.

|Subtype|Content entry|Priority information|Identity/language risks|
|---|---|---|---|
|Chinese opera|Actual work, role/action or tradition|Performer/role, company, session|Confusing traditions or Western opera|
|Xiangsheng|Show concept, partnership, verbal rhythm|Partner relationships, theatre, session|Calling it stand-up; invented humour/relationships|
|Symphony|Programme proposition, ensemble/performance identity|Conductor/soloist/works, venue|Confusing composers and performers; wrong work names|

Anchor the design in a specific work, movement, sound structure or context. Photography, props/material details, title construction or musical rhythm must explain their relationship to the programme. Choose classical or contemporary character from the actual show. Cultural objects, costumes, regional motifs and official identity retain human review.

Separate expressive title and functional copy. Chinese serif, sans and written faces are conditional choices; English serif, humanist or regular sans can all fit. Weight, actual size and background jointly determine readability. Preserve performer, work and company phrases. Read both language strategies and role indexes.

Three readable references: symphony053 and fusion065 are strict; dance109 is a majority adjacent reference. No selected Chinese-opera or Xiangsheng exemplar exists. Those modules cover necessary decisions but remain pending-case; they are not claimed to be trained on absent excellent posters. Update evidence only after real matching cases are reviewed.

Derive campaign, programme detail and session editions only as required. Recheck long programmes, light serif, written titles and performer roles across ratios. Human confirmation of cultural suitability, typography and final direction precedes final status.
