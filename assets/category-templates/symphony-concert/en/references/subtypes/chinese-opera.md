# Chinese opera: tradition, work and performance identity

No selected Chinese-opera poster is present in this bundle. This is a conditional strategy awaiting case validation, not evidence transferred as if dance or symphony were Chinese opera. Establish the actual tradition, complete work/excerpts, work title, role, lead performers, company and production version. Costumes or masks alone do not identify a tradition.

Capture approved title/translation, tradition, company/performer-role relationships, sessions, venue and ticketing; add surtitles, duration and viewing notes only when sourced. Do not invent masters, schools, heritage status or definitive editions. Sourced movement, costume, prop or stage photography can anchor the design without generic mask/dragon/architecture clutter.

Compare role/action and space, work-specific imagery/props and legible title construction. Profile opera-classical compares Source Han Serif and Ma Shan Zheng's written character without making calligraphy mandatory. Keep names, sessions and body text stable.

Prefer company-approved English names. When an explanation is necessary, distinguish Chinese opera/xiqu from Western opera; confirm tradition-specific names. A written Chinese headline can pair with legible English serif or sans rather than imitation calligraphy. Check role identity, costume/props, phrasing, billing and ticketing. Raise evidence status only after reviewing actual matching cases.
